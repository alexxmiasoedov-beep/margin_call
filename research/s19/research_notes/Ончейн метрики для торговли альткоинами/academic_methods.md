# On-chain data as predictors of crypto returns/volatility: academic evidence and how to test on-chain signals without fooling yourself (notes compiled 10 Nov 2026)

Legend used below: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed journal/conference; **[WP]** = working paper/preprint; **[IND]** = industry/vendor research or press; **IS** = in-sample only; **OOS** = some out-of-sample / holdout / later-sample evidence reported. The end reader trades Binance USDT-M altcoin perps, so altcoin- and short-horizon relevance is flagged where it exists (it is often absent: most rigorous on-chain evidence is BTC/ETH-only).

## Q1. Which on-chain predictors have the most robust published evidence for short horizons (hours to weeks), and which failed replication?

### Takeaway
The best-supported short-horizon on-chain signals are (a) exchange-reserve / exchange net-inflow changes for BTC and ETH (inflows to exchanges → lower subsequent returns, and inflows themselves follow price drops), (b) stablecoin minting / USDT net inflows to exchanges → small positive BTC/ETH returns over the next 1–24 hours, and (c) at a weekly cross-sectional horizon, the "crypto value effect" (price relative to new/active addresses), which survived a post-2020 subsample. Every one of these is statistically detectable but economically tiny at intraday horizons (adjusted R² ≈ 0.1–0.2%, minute-level effects of a few basis points), almost entirely BTC/ETH-based or limited to ~100–130 large coins, built on vendor address labels without point-in-time control, and essentially never tested net of trading costs. "Tether prints drive BTC up", "price-to-wallet-users predicts BTC", large-transfer ("whale alert") signals, and—after survivorship correction—momentum and market beta in the cross-section are contested, economically negligible or failed.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Exchange flows / exchange reserves
- **Hoang & Baur (2022), "Loaded for bear: Bitcoin private wallets, exchange reserves and prices", Journal of Banking & Finance 144:106622, doi 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2022.106622 [PR; BTC only]**: investors prefer to hold BTC in private wallets and use exchanges mainly to trade; exchange-reserve changes caused by wallet↔exchange movements are *negatively related to contemporaneous and future* BTC returns (moving coins onto exchanges = more selling pressure, off exchanges = less); flows onto exchanges are themselves *triggered by large negative shocks and increased volatility* (i.e., reverse causality must be controlled). — [UWA repository abstract](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/loaded-for-bear-bitcoin-private-wallets-exchange-reserves-and-pri/); [SSRN 3902504](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3902504)
- **Chi, Chu & Hao, "Return and Volatility Forecasting Using On-Chain Flows in Cryptocurrency Markets" (arXiv 2411.06327, v2 Sep 2025; SSRN 4630115) [WP; BTC, ETH, USDT; IS + claimed OOS]**: sample 16 Dec 2017–20 Jan 2023, flows aggregated over ~20+ exchanges incl. Binance, Bitfinex, Bybit, Coinbase; horizons 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 h. USDT net inflows to exchanges positively predict BTC and ETH returns (significant at 1–2 h, insignificant at 3–6 h); ETH net inflows negatively predict ETH returns at all 1–6 h intervals (e.g., 1-h β = −0.017 per US$1m net inflow, t = −5.81, adjusted R² = 0.001; 6-h β = −0.026, t = −3.75, adj. R² = 0.002); BTC net inflows lack predictive power for BTC returns except at 4 h, but are negatively associated with subsequent volatility at all intervals; USDT inflows predict lower ETH volatility. The paper says it runs OOS tests but reports no OOS R²/Sharpe in the abstract and no cost-adjusted trading backtest (only option "case studies"). — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06327); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4630115)
- Chi et al. cite Hoang & Baur (2020, 2022) as having "confirmed BTC net inflows to forecast negative BTC returns and volatility", i.e., their own BTC null at most horizons partly contradicts the BTC finding at intraday frequency. — [arXiv PDF, intro and references](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06327)
- **Ciaian, Kancs & Rajcaniova (2026), "On- and off-chain demand and supply drivers of Bitcoin price", Economics (De Gruyter) 20(1):1–23 / EERI RP 2026/01 [PR; BTC; daily ARDL 2019–2024]**: ~three quarters of BTC transactions are off-chain; on-chain *demand* pressure is significant short- and long-run, on-chain supply is not; "whale trading" has a weaker long-run effect but a more pronounced contemporaneous and one-lag effect. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/eei/rpaper/eeri_rp_2026_01.html)
- A vendor-blog claim that "a 2025 Coin Metrics study found large exchange inflows predicted sell-offs with 72% accuracy" could not be traced to any original study. — [TradeAlgo guide (unverified claim)](https://www.tradealgo.com/trading-guides/crypto/on-chain-analysis-how-to-use-blockchain-data-to-predict-crypto-price-moves)

#### B. Stablecoin issuance and stablecoin flows
- **Griffin & Shams (2020), "Is Bitcoin Really Untethered?", Journal of Finance 75(4):1913–1964, doi 10.1111/jofi.12903 [PR; 2017–18 boom; IS]**: using blockchain flow data, Tether purchases were timed after BTC downturns and followed by sizable BTC price increases; flows attributable to one large entity on Bitfinex. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12903)
- **Wei (2018), "The impact of Tether grants on Bitcoin", Economics Letters [PR; daily data]**: Tether grants had no statistically significant impact on BTC returns, though grants followed BTC downturns and raised trading volume. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176518302556)
- **Ante, Fiedler & Strehle (2021), "The influence of stablecoin issuances on cryptocurrency markets", Finance Research Letters 41 [PR; hourly event study; IS]**: 565 issuances ≥ US$1m of seven stablecoins (Apr 2019–Mar 2020); market *downturns in the week before* issuance; positive CAARs of 0.31–0.47% over −12…−1 h and 0.47–0.69% over 0…+24 h for major coins, 0.72% (XRP) to 1.1% (LTC) over the whole window; issuance size *not* significant; USDT effect shows up *before* the event; authors suggest peg-arbitrage as a mechanism. USDT issuance was split 60% TRON / 38% Ethereum / ~1% each Omni and EOS. — [BRL WP 11 PDF](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The_Influence_of_Stablecoin_Issuances_on_Cryptocurrency_Markets_BRL_Working_Paper_No_11.pdf); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v41y2021ics1544612320316810.html)
- **Saggu (2022), Finance Research Letters 49 [PR; 2014–2021; intraday]**: BTC's reaction to Tether *minting* is positive but fades after about an hour; burns do not produce symmetric negative reactions; reaction stronger in bull/greed regimes; Whale Alert tweets significantly amplify the minting effect. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v49y2022ics154461232200321x.html); [BRL summary](https://blockchainresearchlab.org/2022/07/29/the-role-of-whale-alerts-and-sentiment-in-bitcoins-reaction-to-minting-and-burning-of-tethers-usdt)
- **Kristoufek (2021), "Tethered, or Untethered? On the interplay between stablecoins and major cryptoassets", Finance Research Letters 43:101991 [PR]** and Kristoufek (2022), Financial Innovation: no evidence that stablecoins boost other cryptoassets' prices; causality runs from crypto prices to stablecoin issuance (issuance reflects demand). — [IDEAS (FRL)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v43y2021ics1544612321000726.html); [IDEAS (Financial Innovation)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/fininn/v8y2022i1d10.1186_s40854-022-00343-8.html)

#### C. Large ("whale") transfers and holder-size classes
- **Ante & Fiedler (2021), "Market reaction to large transfers on the Bitcoin blockchain – do size and motive matter?", Finance Research Letters 39:101619 [PR; minute-level event study; IS]**: 2,132 transactions ≥ 500 BTC (Sep 2018–Nov 2019), Gemini minute prices, mean-return model with a 2-hour estimation window. Average AR in the minute after confirmation −0.0067% (t = −2.52); CAAR over 0…+15 min +0.026%; *no size effect* across quartiles. By motive: deposits to exchange cold wallets (n = 51) CAAR −0.34% over −15…+15 min; cold-wallet withdrawals (n = 129) +0.075%; large *hot-wallet deposits* (n = 612, Bitfinex/Binance) did **not** produce the expected negative reaction (−0.029% in the 16 minutes *before*, +0.009% at t+2); non-exchange transfers (n = 434) −0.027% at t+2. Effects are in hundredths of a percent and gone within minutes. — [BRL WP 9 PDF](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v39y2021ics1544612320304438.html)
- **Chernoff & Jagtiani, "Beneath the Crypto Currents: The Hidden Effect of Crypto Whales" (Philadelphia Fed WP 24-14; Journal of Credit Risk) [WP/PR; ETH only]**: large ETH holders add to positions before price rises while small holders reduce theirs; ETH return volatility appears driven by small retail holders rather than whales. — [Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/the-economy/banking-and-financial-markets/beneath-the-crypto-currents-the-hidden-effect-of-crypto-whales); [Journal of Credit Risk](https://www.risk.net/journal-of-credit-risk/7961816/beneath-the-crypto-currents-the-hidden-effect-of-crypto-%E2%80%9Cwhales%E2%80%9D)
- **Herremans & Low (2022), arXiv 2211.08281 [WP; BTC; Jan 2018–Sep 2021]**: CryptoQuant on-chain/exchange/miner data plus Whale Alert tweets used in a transformer model to forecast next-day BTC *volatility spikes*; claims to beat benchmarks and to reduce drawdown in simple strategies (deep-learning, short sample, no point-in-time data control described). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08281)

#### D. Network activity, new/active addresses (weekly cross-section)
- **Liu & Tsyvinski (2021), "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", Review of Financial Studies 34(6):2689–2727, doi 10.1093/rfs/hhaa113 [PR; BTC/ETH/XRP]**: returns are *contemporaneously* exposed to network factors (wallet users, active addresses, transactions, payments) but not to production/mining factors; coin returns *predict* subsequent wallet-user growth (reverse direction); the robust return predictors are time-series momentum and investor attention (Google/Twitter). — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v34y2021i6p2689-2727..html); [SSRN 3226806](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3226806)
  - In the NBER version, the price-to-"dividend" ratio (price / Bitcoin wallet users) has **no** predictive power for daily BTC returns 1–7 days ahead (coefficients insignificant, R² = 0.00); a 1-SD rise in today's BTC return predicts +0.33% tomorrow; a 1-SD rise in "Bitcoin" Google searches predicts +2.3% two-week-ahead returns. — [NBER w24877 PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)
- **Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Accounting for Cryptocurrency Value" (WP, Oct 2021) [WP; 127 coins, Feb 2018–Jun 2021, weekly; Glassnode data]**: innovations in new addresses explain ~8% of cross-sectional return variation; strong reaction at the (weekly) release of new-address information with *no* pre- or post-drift; a long-short portfolio buying low and shorting high price-to-new-address coins earns 1.9% per week, alpha significant at 1% against the crypto 3-factor model, still significant (10%) 20 weeks after formation. — [Cowles/Yale PDF](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/onchain_v18.pdf)
- **Borri, Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2026), "Cryptocurrency as an Investable Asset Class: Coming of Age", Annual Review of Financial Economics 18 (arXiv 2510.14435) [PR review; sub-period, not true OOS]**: on CoinGecko data Dec 2013–Sep 2025 (18,622 active + 29,230 delisted coins), the price-to-new-address value spread (Q5−Q1, weekly) is −0.035 (t = −3.50) full sample and −0.023 (t = −2.32) post-2020; momentum post-2020 0.021 (t = 3.70); size −0.009 (t = −1.69); other "zoo" signals (12/24-month momentum, volatility, volume, betas) insignificant. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- **Cong, Karolyi, Tang & Zhao, "Value Premium, Network Adoption, and Factor Pricing of Crypto Assets" (WP 2021/22) [WP → PR]**: 4,007 crypto assets; a five-factor model (market, size, momentum, value, network adoption) said to price the cross-section in- and out-of-sample. Published version (per INFORMS listing) "Crypto Value, Factor Pricing, and Market Segmentation", Management Science, doi 10.1287/mnsc.2024.05875, builds value on the *active-addresses-to-market-cap* ratio and interprets the premium as compensation for on-chain activity risk. A conference discussant noted the value factor behaves like long-horizon return reversal. — [WP PDF](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/690326/CKTZ_CryptoFactors_0907_RoF-format.pdf); [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05875); [ABFER discussion slides](https://abfer.org/media/abfer-events-2022/annual-conference/slides-investfin/Yukun-LIU-discussion.pdf)
- **DEX order flow**: Capponi, Jia & Yu, "Price Discovery on Decentralized Exchanges", Review of Financial Studies (2025/26, doi 10.1093/rfs/hhag002) [PR]: high-priority-fee DEX trades carry more private information and contribute more to price discovery; earlier version used Ethereum mempool data. Relevant for tokens whose primary liquidity is on-chain. — [SMU repository record](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/7508); [SMU PDF](https://skbi.smu.edu.sg/sites/skbi.smu.edu.sg/files/Research/price-discovery-on-decentralized-exchange.pdf)

#### E. Failed, contested, fragile or withdrawn results
- **Tether → BTC**: Griffin & Shams (positive, 2017–18) vs. Wei 2018 (no effect on returns) and Kristoufek 2021/2022 (issuance *follows* prices) — unresolved; even supportive event studies show issuance follows market downturns. — [Griffin & Shams](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12903); [Wei](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176518302556); [Kristoufek](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v43y2021ics1544612321000726.html)
- **Price-to-wallet-users (NVT-like ratio)**: no predictive power for BTC (R² = 0.00). — [NBER w24877](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)
- **Survivorship destroys some "anomalies"**: Ammann, Liebi, Burdorf & Stöckl (WP, Mar 2022), 3,904 coins 2014–2021: annualized survivorship bias 0.93% (value-weighted) and 62.19% (equal-weighted); size premium significantly inflated in survivor samples; after correcting for survivorship and delisting bias they find *no* positive relation between average returns and momentum or market beta. — [Stöckl publication page](https://diffform.netlify.app/publication/2022_liebi_survivorship)
- **Non-standard errors**: Fieberg, Günther, Poddig & Zaremba (2024), International Review of Financial Analysis 92:103106 — across 20,736 research designs × 43 sorting variables, the dispersion of results caused by researcher choices ("non-standard errors") is larger than in stocks and exceeds standard errors; reducing the influence of the smallest coins reduces it; size and momentum are the most robust. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v92y2024ics1057521924000383.html); [Borri et al. review](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- **Large-transfer signals are economically negligible** (basis-point, minute-scale; hot-wallet deposits not negative). — [Ante & Fiedler WP](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf)
- **BTC net inflows at intraday horizons**: mostly no return predictability (except 4 h). — [Chi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- **Withdrawn result (data-pipeline error)**: Kamat, arXiv 2607.02795 (Jul 2026) on pump.fun "sniper cohorts" (1.58m buyer observations, 166,098 launches, PSM-matched +16.1% first-30-min buyer lift; its own placebo estimator was biased, median +189.6%) — Oct 2026 correction: buyer records included sells and missed many buys, *all numerical results withdrawn*. — [arXiv abstract page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)
- **Contested pre-pump accumulation timing**: Karbalaii (arXiv 2504.15790, Apr 2025; Poloniex, 1,021 tokens) reports 70% of pre-event volume within one hour of the pump and median insider returns >100%; a review argues the method labels any non-zero pre-target trading as "insider accumulation", that 30.7% of events had no pre-pump trading, and that the reported mean accumulation span (2,160.8 min) contradicts the "final hour" headline. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.15790); [token-research.com critique](https://token-research.com/research/tr-984)
- **Multiple-testing correction does not automatically kill technical signals on large coins**: Hudson & Urquhart (2021), Annals of Operations Research 297:191–220 (doi 10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1) test ~15,000 technical rules on BTC, LTC, ETH, XRP (daily) and report significant predictability surviving multiple-hypothesis procedures — but only on 4 large coins and before costs. — [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715)

### Inferences
- Ranking by robustness for a short-horizon altcoin perp trader: (1) exchange-inflow → lower returns has the best peer-reviewed support but only for BTC (daily) and ETH (1–6 h); (2) stablecoin mint / USDT-inflow → higher majors' returns over hours is supported by several event studies but contradicted on causality and decays within ~1 hour (Saggu) to ~24 hours (Ante et al.); (3) weekly cross-sectional "value" (price ÷ new or active addresses) is the only on-chain signal with a post-sample re-test across many coins, but it is a slow (weekly, 20-week persistent) characteristic, not an hours-to-days trigger.
- The pattern "inflows follow price drops" (Hoang & Baur; Ante et al.'s pre-issuance downturns; Wei) means any naive flow signal is partly a lagged-return signal; tests must control for lagged returns and volatility (Chi et al.'s two-variable model is the minimum).
- Typical intraday effect sizes (adj. R² ≈ 0.001–0.002; minute ARs of −0.0067%) are an order of magnitude below a perp round-trip taker cost, so "statistically significant" here does not imply tradable.
- Nothing in the peer-reviewed literature establishes that BTC/ETH flow results transfer to small-cap altcoins; for altcoins, the closest evidence is cross-sectional weekly (≈127 Glassnode-covered coins) or event-based (unlocks, listings, pumps — see Q2/Q3).
- The withdrawn pump.fun paper and the contested Karbalaii paper show that on-chain microstructure claims are fragile to data-construction errors; replication on independently built data is necessary before trading.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that tests exchange-flow or holder-flow signals across hundreds of altcoins at hourly–daily horizons with point-in-time labels and transaction costs.
- No academic OOS validation found (in this search) of popular vendor metrics such as NVT, MVRV, SOPR, "exchange whale ratio" for altcoins; I did not specifically search all of them, so this is "not found", not "does not exist".
- Chi et al.'s OOS statistics and the exact vendor of their flow data are not reported in the parts read; Hoang & Baur's effect sizes and horizons were not retrieved (abstract only).
- The Coin Metrics "72% accuracy" claim could not be traced to a primary source.

## Q2. What do papers find about exchange-deposit flows of large holders or project teams preceding price declines, and about token unlock events (and airdrops)?

### Takeaway
For BTC, peer-reviewed evidence (Hoang & Baur 2022) links movements of coins onto exchanges to lower future returns, while large individual deposits ("whale alerts") have only basis-point, minute-scale effects; for ETH a working paper finds negative 1–6 h predictability from net inflows. There is **no** peer-reviewed systematic study of *project-team* wallet deposits preceding declines—evidence is anecdotal (e.g., Mantra/OM, April 2025) and depends on disputed wallet labels. Token-unlock evidence is overwhelmingly industry research (Keyrock 16,000+ events: ~90% negative, declines begin ~30 days before, team unlocks worst) plus one small preliminary SSRN paper on Binance-listed tokens; airdropped tokens typically decline in the first months. None of the unlock/airdrop studies uses matched controls, point-in-time unlock calendars, or cost-adjusted backtests.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Exchange deposits by large holders
- Hoang & Baur (JBF 2022): transfers onto exchanges imply increased selling pressure and are negatively related to contemporaneous and *future* BTC returns; inflows are triggered by large negative shocks and higher volatility. — [UWA abstract](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/loaded-for-bear-bitcoin-private-wallets-exchange-reserves-and-pri/)
- Chi, Chu & Hao (WP 2025): ETH net inflows negatively predict ETH returns at 1–6 h; BTC net inflows mostly do not predict BTC returns intraday. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327)
- Ante & Fiedler (FRL 2021): large (≥ 500 BTC) deposits to exchange hot wallets did *not* lead to negative returns; deposits to cold wallets were followed by −0.34% over ±15 minutes (n = 51). — [BRL WP 9](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf)
- Chernoff & Jagtiani (Philadelphia Fed WP 24-14): ETH whales accumulate *before* price rises and small holders sell before rises. — [Philadelphia Fed](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/the-economy/banking-and-financial-markets/beneath-the-crypto-currents-the-hidden-effect-of-crypto-whales)
- **Why raw "exchange inflow" is a noisy selling-intent proxy** — Makarov & Schoar, "Blockchain Analysis of the Bitcoin Market" (NBER WP 29396; version 18 Apr 2022) [WP]: ~90% of nominal on-chain BTC volume is not economically meaningful (protocol by-products, anonymity chains); since 2015, 75% of *real* BTC volume is linked to exchanges or exchange-like entities (on-line wallets, OTC desks, large institutional traders); for a typical exchange, 53% of its BTC outflow goes to other exchanges and 52% of its inflow comes from other exchanges (arbitrage/rebalancing, not end-investor selling); top 10,000 entities held ~4.8–5m BTC (Jan 2021). — [MIT Sloan PDF](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Bitcoin-blockchain%20-%20AER.pdf); [NBER listing](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:nbr:nberwo:29396)
- Hautsch, Scheuch & Voigt (2024), "Building Trust Takes Time: Limits to Arbitrage for Blockchain-Based Assets", Review of Finance 28(4):1345–1381, doi 10.1093/rof/rfae004 [PR]: settlement latency of on-chain transfers creates arbitrage bounds averaging 121 bp that explain 91% of cross-exchange BTC price differences, and asset flows *chase arbitrage opportunities* — i.e., a large share of exchange in/outflows is arbitrage inventory. — [arXiv 1812.00595](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00595); [RevFin](https://revfin.org/?p=3609)
- **Insiders pre-positioning before listing announcements (on-chain footprint)**: Félez-Viñas, Johnson & Putniņš, "Insider Trading in Cryptocurrency Markets" (SSRN 4184367, Aug 2022) [WP, not peer-reviewed at time of coverage]: Coinbase listings Sep 2018–May 2022; using public blockchain records of pre-announcement trades, insider trading is estimated in 10–25% of listings (lower bound), insiders' profits ≥ US$1.5m; significant pre-announcement price run-ups. — [UTS news](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2022/08/insider-trading-crypto-markets); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/107671/coinbase-insider-trading-problem-study)
- **Team/insider-linked deposits before a crash (case, not a study)**: before the ~90% OM (Mantra) crash on 13 Apr 2025, Lookonchain reported ≥ 17 wallets depositing 43.6m OM (4.5% of circulating supply) to exchanges from 7 April; Spot On Chain reported 14.27m OM moved to OKX three days before; investor Laser Digital denied the wallets were theirs, and Mantra's CEO said Arkham "mislabeled" wallets. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/mantra-investors-dumped-om-before-90-percent-crash); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/350717/mantra-laser-digital-om-token-crash); [Arkham-mislabel report (Cointelegraph repost)](https://coworking.nanabianca.it/?p=707018)

#### B. Token unlocks / vesting
- **Keyrock, "From Locked to Liquidity: What 16,000+ Token Unlocks Teach Us" [IND; market maker/OTC firm]**: 16,000+ unlock events across 40 tokens (linear tranches treated as separate events), daily prices ±30 days, normalized against ETH; ~90% of unlocks show negative price pressure; impact often starts ~30 days before the unlock, accelerating in the final week; team unlocks worst (cited −25%); investor unlocks (106 events) slow and mild (attributed to OTC/TWAP/options hedging — inferred, not observed); ecosystem-development unlocks the only positive category (+1.18%); linear vesting less disruptive than cliffs; large cliffs often recover after 30 days; data source and period not disclosed. — [Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/)
- **Kim (2026), "The 72-Hour Shock? Preliminary Evidence from 52 Token Unlock Events on Binance" (SSRN 6632838) [WP, preliminary]**: 52 unlocks of Binance-listed tokens, Jan 2023–Dec 2025; 46/52 (88.5%) negative within 72 h, mean −16.97%; exploratory note that all 14 events within 60 days of the 365-day post-listing anniversary were negative (hypothesis-generating); no benchmark model stated in the abstract. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6632838)
- **Tokenomist [IND]**: one-month pre-event median −14.7% (n = 164), explicitly descriptive/correlational; one of its controls was null; argues unlock size relative to a thin float matters more than recipient type; counterexample — ApeCoin's monthly cliffs saw ~+16% (size-weighted) in the 15 days *before* each cliff. — [Tokenomist glossary](https://tokenomist.ai/learn/glossary-term/unlock-price-impact)
- **6th Man Ventures [IND]**: ~5,000 unlocks; no meaningful relationship for unlocks < 1% of supply, negative relationship above. — [6th Man Ventures](https://6thman.ventures/writing/token-unlocks)
- **Practitioner backtest [IND/blog]**: shorting high forward-dilution unlocks — effect fades by 14–21 days; author did not trade it after costs. — [Medium/Coinmonks](https://medium.com/coinmonks/i-backtested-shorting-token-unlocks-heres-why-i-m-not-trading-it-yet-42e237d40d9a)

#### C. Airdrops
- Hashed (31 airdrops, Uniswap → Evmos) [IND]: by day 100 the median airdropped token traded at 0.64× its day-1 price (−36%), 74% below launch price; median 0.44× by day 200. — [Hashed](https://medium.com/hashed-official/we-need-to-rethink-airdrops-next-cycle-730706f963a7)
- Keyrock (62 airdrops in 2024; "11% success rate") [IND]: most of the price move happens in the first days after the airdrop; smaller airdrops have less initial selling but often dump within months. — [Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/airdrops-in-the-barren-desert/)
- arXiv 2312.02752 [WP]: nine major Ethereum/L2 airdrops; short-term activity spikes (Arbitrum case) do not translate into sustained usage; Sybil farming is a core design flaw. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02752)

### Inferences
- For BTC, "coins moving onto exchanges" is a defensible bearish input at daily horizons, but a large share of measured inflow is inter-exchange arbitrage inventory and internal reshuffles (Makarov & Schoar; Hautsch et al.; Glassnode, see Q4), so a team/whale-deposit signal for altcoins must be built from *identified* source wallets (vesting contracts, team/VC/treasury wallets) rather than aggregate exchange inflow.
- The unlock literature agrees on direction (negative, pre-event drift beginning weeks before) but every quantitative estimate comes from vendors with undisclosed data or from n = 52; pre-event drift means the tradable window is *before* the unlock, and the main confound is the general altcoin downtrend over 2022–2025 (ETH-normalization only partly addresses it). A matched-control event study (same-date non-unlocking tokens with similar size/momentum/funding) is the missing piece.
- Because unlock calendars are public and pre-event declines start ~30 days early, perp funding and crowded shorts could absorb the edge; none of the sources measured funding.
- Label risk is central: the OM case shows that "team wallet → exchange" attributions can be disputed even after the fact; a backtest that uses today's labels for 2025 events is look-ahead-biased.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, systematic study found of project-team or VC wallet deposits to exchanges preceding altcoin declines.
- No peer-reviewed token-unlock event study with matched controls, abnormal returns vs. a market/sector benchmark, or perp funding/costs; Keyrock's underlying data and period are undisclosed.
- No academic study found on market-maker token-loan/option deals and their on-chain footprint (e.g., market-maker dumping after listing).
- Hoang & Baur's exact horizons and magnitudes not retrieved.

## Q3. What do papers on crypto market manipulation (pump-and-dump, wash trading, spoofing, insider trading) say about on-chain footprints that precede manipulation events?

### Takeaway
For exchange-coordinated pump-and-dumps, the robust precursor is *market* data (abnormal returns/volume concentrated in the last hour before the announcement, small market caps), which predicts target coins with AUC > 0.9 out-of-sample (Xu & Livshits). Genuinely *on-chain* precursors are documented for DEX/memecoin manipulation and rug pulls (creator/sniper accumulation in the creation block, liquidity-pool mint/burn patterns, concentrated holdings — rug pulls classifiable before the exit with ~99% accuracy on imbalanced data) and for insider trading before listing announcements (10–25% of Coinbase listings). Wash trading is detectable statistically (Benford, round-size clustering, log-normality, structural breaks; power-law tests unreliable) and on-chain (self-trades, account rings), and inflates volume massively on unregulated venues (>70%). No literature was found on on-chain footprints of spoofing (an order-book behaviour).

### Cited Findings

#### A. Pump-and-dump (Telegram/exchange-coordinated)
- **Xu & Livshits (2019), "The Anatomy of a Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dump Scheme", USENIX Security (arXiv 1811.10109) [PR; OOS]**: 412 pumps from Telegram channels (Jun 2018–Feb 2019); anomalous returns appear *before* the admins announce the coin, most concentrated one hour before (consistent with organizers buying first); random-forest models predict the target coin with AUC > 0.9 in- and out-of-sample (RF AUC 0.948–0.954); a strategy using the model returned 60% over 11 weeks on small retail size; admins' estimated net profit 199.52 BTC over 348 pumps, insiders' average return ~18%; half of pumped coins had market cap < 100 BTC. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10109)
- **Li, Shin & Wang, "Cryptocurrency Pump-and-Dump Schemes", Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis (online 2025) [PR]**: P&Ds organized via Telegram cause short-lived spikes in price, volume and volatility followed by quick reversals; uses Bittrex's Nov 2017 P&D ban as a natural experiment; working-paper versions document pre-pump trading (exact figures differ across versions). — [Cambridge/JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/cryptocurrency-pumpanddump-schemes/97149DCD519BAF838F269F98DC76D682)
- **Dhawan & Putniņš (2023), "A New Wolf in Town? Pump-and-Dump Manipulation in Cryptocurrency Markets", Review of Finance 27(3):935–975 [PR]**: Binance and Yobit data plus 72 Telegram pump channels, 350+ P&Ds in seven months; addresses why people join negative-expected-value schemes (overconfidence, gambling). — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:revfin:v:27:y:2023:i:3:p:935-975.); [UTS news](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2021/09/beware-wolf-cryptocurrency-markets)
- **La Morgia, Mei, Sassi & Stefa (2023), "The Doge of Wall Street", ACM Transactions on Internet Technology (arXiv 2105.00733) [PR]**: >1,000 confirmed pumps by 20 Telegram groups; detection ~25 seconds after the pump starts with F1 = 94.5% (vs. 30 minutes and 62.7% for the earlier Kamps & Kleinberg approach). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00733)
- Fantazzini & Xiao (2023), Econometrics 11(3):22 [PR]: detection models must handle heavy class imbalance and insiders' *anticipated purchases* before the announcement. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2225-1146/11/3/22)

#### B. DEX / memecoin manipulation and rug pulls (true on-chain footprints)
- **Ding, Luo & Xu, "Decompose Market Manipulation Strategies: Evidence from On-chain Meme Coin Market" (SBFC 2025 WP)**: 6,000 pump.fun meme coins with on-chain and comment data; identifies "rat" and "sniper" concealed accumulation/front-running, wash trading and comment bots; creators/snipers profit from low-cost concealed accumulation and timing the dump; wash trading and fake comments attract traders; inventory concentration and attention fabrication drive participation and wealth redistribution. — [SBFC PDF](https://sbfc.sydney.edu.au/2025/papers/SBFC2025_4G3_P154.pdf)
- **Mazorra, Adan & Daza (2022), "Do Not Rug on Me", Mathematics 10(6):949 [PR]**: 26,957 scam/rug-pull tokens vs. 631 non-malicious Uniswap tokens; features from LP-token mint/burn and transfer events, pool state and token distribution (holder concentration); best model accuracy 0.9936, recall 0.954, precision 0.984 in separating scams *before* the malicious manoeuvre (note the extreme class imbalance). — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-7390/10/6/949); [arXiv 2201.07220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.07220)
- A 2025 preprint shows "fragmented" rug pulls (exit volume split across many small trades and non-owner addresses) evade classic three-condition heuristics on six Ethereum DEXs. — [arXiv 2511.15463](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15463.pdf)
- Chainalysis [IND]: in 2023, 54% of ERC-20 tokens launched on DEXs showed pump-and-dump-like patterns but they accounted for only 1.3% of DEX volume; in 2024, 4.52% of new tokens (~137k) showed P&D characteristics and suspected DEX wash trading reached up to US$2.57bn (heuristic estimates; Chainalysis cautions they are not legal determinations). — [Decrypt (2023 data)](https://decrypt.co/215205/54-of-tokens-appear-to-be-pump-and-dumps-but-theres-good-news); [Economy Middle East (2024 wash trading)](https://economymiddleeast.com/news/suspected-wash-trading-blockchains-2-57-billion-2024-report); [CriptoFácil (2024 P&D tokens)](https://www.criptofacil.com/fraudes-em-defi-relatorio-aponta-137-mil-tokens-suspeitos-de-pump-and-dump)

#### C. Wash trading
- **Cong, Li, Tang & Yang (2023), "Crypto Wash Trading", Management Science 69(11):6427–6454 [PR]**: 29 centralized exchanges; regulated exchanges show trade-size and first-digit patterns seen in normal markets; unregulated exchanges fail Benford first-significant-digit, round-size clustering and tail-distribution tests; average wash trading > 70% of reported volume on unregulated exchanges. — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:69:y:2023:i:11:p:6427-6454); [NBER w30783](https://nber.org/papers/w30783)
- **Aloosh & Li, "Direct Evidence of Bitcoin Wash Trading", Management Science (2024) [PR]**: leaked internal Mt. Gox records (2011–2013) give direct evidence of wash trading (mostly self-trades by the same account); validates indirect detectors — Benford's law, trade-size clustering, log-normality and structural breaks are useful, while *power-law tail* measures give the opposite conclusion. — [DCU repository PDF](https://doras.dcu.ie/32898/1/Direct%20evidence%20of%20bitcoin%20wash%20trading.pdf)
- **Le Pennec, Fiedler & Ante (2021), "Wash trading at cryptocurrency exchanges" [venue believed to be Finance Research Letters; not verified in this search]**: compares reported volumes with on-chain exchange wallet balances and web traffic (Jul–Nov 2019) to flag exchanges (e.g., FCoin, Huobi, OKEx then); per a Quantpedia summary, 96–98% of reported volume at a group of suspicious exchanges is highly questionable. — [Decrypt coverage](https://decrypt.co/39448/researchers-call-on-coinmarketcap-to-highlight-wash-trading); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/detecting-wash-trading-in-major-crypto-exchanges/)
- **Victor & Weintraud (2021), "Detecting and Quantifying Wash Trading on Decentralized Cryptocurrency Exchanges", WWW '21 [PR]**: on IDEX and EtherDelta, >30% of traded tokens were subject to wash trading; ~10% of EtherDelta tokens almost exclusively wash-traded; multi-account structures on IDEX suggest deliberate obfuscation. — [arXiv 2102.07001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07001v1)
- **Enforcement — DOJ "Operation Token Mirrors" (Oct 2024)**: FBI created a bait token (NexFundAI); 18 individuals/entities charged, including "market makers" ZM Quant, CLS Global and MyTrade (wash trading the FBI token) and Gotbit; SEC parallel actions. Further indictments on 30 Mar 2026 (N.D. Cal.) charged ten foreign nationals at Gotbit, Vortex, Antier and Contrarian (per TRM Labs). — [DOJ USAO-MA](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/eighteen-individuals-and-entities-charged-international-operation-targeting-widespread); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/285503/feds-charge-gotbit-others-market-manipulation); [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/ten-fraudsters-from-four-financial-services-firms-charged-in-different-cryptocurrency-market-manipulation-schemes-out-of-northern-district-of-california)

#### D. Insider trading and stablecoin-based price support
- Félez-Viñas, Johnson & Putniņš (SSRN 2022): on-chain wallets trading before Coinbase listing announcements; insider trading in 10–25% of listings. — [UTS news](https://www.uts.edu.au/news/2022/08/insider-trading-crypto-markets)
- Griffin & Shams (JF 2020): on-chain Tether flows from one large Bitfinex entity, timed after BTC downturns, preceded BTC price increases (2017–18). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12903)

### Inferences
- For Binance perps, the transferable lesson from P&D research is *market-data* based: abnormal spot/perp volume and returns in the hour before an event, small float, and thin books; on-chain accumulation signals are most informative where the token's primary liquidity is on-chain (new tokens, memecoins, pre-listing DEX trading).
- Holder concentration and creator/insider inventory are the recurring on-chain precursors across rug-pull and memecoin studies; this is consistent with the unlock/team-deposit concern in Q2 but has not been tested as a return predictor for listed altcoin perps.
- Reported spot volume used as a control or denominator is contaminated by wash trading (especially off-Binance and on DEXs); use exchange-specific volume from venues with regulated/consistent statistics, or on-chain-verified flows.
- Detection accuracy figures (AUC 0.9+, accuracy 0.99) come from imbalanced, curated samples and rarely translate into tradable edge after the event is public; their trading value lies in *avoiding* manipulated names or fading post-pump reversals.

### Gaps
- No academic work found on on-chain footprints of spoofing/layering (it is an order-book phenomenon not recorded on-chain).
- No study found linking market-maker wallet flows (token loans, call options, exchange deposits by market makers) to subsequent altcoin perp returns.
- Final JFQA numbers for Li, Shin & Wang (pre-pump volume share) not verified; Chainalysis primary reports not accessed (figures from press).

## Q4. What practical checklist emerges for testing an on-chain signal on a few hundred altcoins with 1–2 years of data? (pitfalls, remedies and research designs)

### Takeaway
The literature points to eight failure modes specific to on-chain backtests — retroactive labels/clustering (look-ahead), internal and inter-exchange transfers, timestamp/publication lags, incomplete chain coverage, survivorship and bad price/volume data, data snooping, regime shifts, and costs/capacity — and to standard remedies: point-in-time data, matched-control event studies with date-clustering-robust tests, placebo dates/wallets, walk-forward OOS with purging, family-wise or FDR multiple-testing corrections (White RC, Hansen SPA, Romano–Wolf, deflated Sharpe, PBO, BH), block-permutation tests, and explicit cost/capacity modelling. With 1–2 years of data, only very strong effects can clear a multiple-testing-adjusted hurdle.

### Cited Findings

#### Pitfall 1 — Look-ahead bias from retroactive labels and entity clustering
- Glassnode: address clustering is the main source of full-history revisions; new addresses are added to clusters later, so "a transaction that was previously recognized as a transfer between two different parties may suddenly become an internal transfer"; manual label additions recompute full history; for quantitative use "one has to expect information leakage from future clustering knowledge into data of old timestamps", which "introduces a look-ahead bias" — use point-in-time (PiT, append-only, frozen at release) data for all backtests. Full historic recomputation runs daily at 00:30 UTC with release 01:10–02:40 UTC; about two-thirds of new BTC 10-minute points are final at release, ~85% complete within 30 minutes; BTC blocks take ~30–60 min (ETH ~15 min) to enter metrics; state-based entity metrics (illiquid supply, number of whales) still move >1% over the last ~30 days, most changes within 3 months, full convergence ~4 years. — [Glassnode, Introducing Point-in-Time Data](https://research.glassnode.com/introducing-point-in-time-data/); [Glassnode, "Your Backtest Is Lying"](https://research.glassnode.com/why-use-point-in-time-data/)
- Glassnode's exchange-data notice: exchange balances may undergo retrospective revisions when addresses are identified and added; some PiT chart pages still say the most recent points may revise as labels update. — [Exchange Data Transparency Notice](https://docs.glassnode.com/further-information/exchange-data-transparency-notice); [PiT chart example](https://studio.glassnode.com/charts/distribution.BalanceExchangesRelativePit?a=BTC)
- Clustering is a lower bound and labels are incomplete by design: the multi-input heuristic only lower-bounds the number of entities; WalletExplorer covered < 280 entities and missed or under-covered the largest exchanges (Binance, Coinbase, OKEx, FTX, Bitfinex); Makarov & Schoar had to classify 4,507 unlabelled clusters as "likely exchanges" by behaviour (63% of BTC flowing to the top 10,000 clusters). — [Makarov & Schoar PDF](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Bitcoin-blockchain%20-%20AER.pdf)
- Kappos et al. (2022), "How to Peel a Million", USENIX Security: co-spend (multi-input) clustering can merge unrelated users when CoinJoin transactions are present. The authors validate on a constructed ground-truth set of 120 co-spend clusters from Chainalysis data: 60 true positives and 60 false positives containing CoinJoins (a benchmark, not a prevalence estimate). Their cluster-validation classifier reaches 89% accuracy, and their cluster-expansion method has a 12.7% false discovery rate. — [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/kappos)
- Foundational clustering method: Meiklejohn et al. (2013), "A Fistful of Bitcoins", IMC, doi 10.1145/2504730.2504747. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/2504730.2504747)
- Third-party label tools can be low-confidence: in the OM case, Mantra's CEO said Arkham mislabeled wallets. — [report](https://coworking.nanabianca.it/?p=707018)

#### Pitfall 2 — Internal exchange transfers, inter-exchange arbitrage, custody
- Glassnode (22 Apr 2021): "A sudden outflow of 10k BTC from an exchange can turn out to be simply an internal transfer"; internal transfers are much larger than customer flows; new cold wallets may not be detected immediately; precision is preferred over recall; one exchange cluster had ~25m addresses but < 100k with non-zero balance; outflows may go to custody/OTC/collateral; single large flows can be retracted once identified as internal. — [Glassnode, "Exchange Metrics: The Good, The Bad, The Ugly"](https://research.glassnode.com/exchange-metrics)
- ~half of a typical exchange's BTC in/outflow is to/from other exchanges (Makarov & Schoar), and flows chase arbitrage spreads (Hautsch et al.). — [Makarov & Schoar](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Bitcoin-blockchain%20-%20AER.pdf); [Hautsch et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00595)
- Hot vs. cold wallet direction changes the sign of observed reactions (Ante & Fiedler's five motive clusters). — [BRL WP 9](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf)

#### Pitfall 3 — Timestamp alignment and publication lags
- Bitcoin block timestamps are loosely constrained: valid if greater than the median of the previous 11 blocks and less than network-adjusted time + 2 hours. — [Bitcoin Wiki: Block timestamp](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Block_timestamp)
- Transactions are visible in the mempool before confirmation and block intervals are random, so the "event time" is ambiguous; Ante & Fiedler use confirmation time and find pre-event movements. — [BRL WP 9](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf)
- Vendor data arrive with lags (BTC 30–60 min into metrics; daily recomputation released 01:10–02:40 UTC). — [Glassnode PiT article](https://research.glassnode.com/introducing-point-in-time-data/)
- Settlement latency between chain and exchange crediting is economically material (121 bp average arbitrage bounds). — [Hautsch, Scheuch & Voigt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.00595)

#### Pitfall 4 — Chain coverage, multi-chain tokens, off-chain volume
- Stablecoin issuance was spread across chains (USDT: 60% TRON, 38% Ethereum, ~1% Omni/EOS in 2019–20), so single-chain flow measures miss most of the activity. — [Ante, Fiedler & Strehle WP](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The_Influence_of_Stablecoin_Issuances_on_Cryptocurrency_Markets_BRL_Working_Paper_No_11.pdf)
- ~three quarters of BTC transactions are off-chain (exchange-internal), invisible to on-chain metrics. — [Ciaian, Kancs & Rajcaniova (2026)](https://ideas.repec.org/p/eei/rpaper/eeri_rp_2026_01.html)
- ~90% of nominal BTC on-chain volume is not economically meaningful (anonymity chains, protocol by-products). — [Makarov & Schoar](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-06/Bitcoin-blockchain%20-%20AER.pdf)

#### Pitfall 5 — Survivorship, dead/delisted assets, bad price and volume data
- Survivorship bias: 0.93% p.a. (VW) and 62.19% p.a. (EW) on 3,904 coins (2014–2021); size premium inflated; momentum/beta relations vanish after correction. — [Ammann et al. (2022)](https://diffform.netlify.app/publication/2022_liebi_survivorship)
- Best-practice universe: Borri et al. include 29,230 delisted coins alongside 18,622 active ones, require market cap ≥ US$1m at formation, ≥ 30 daily observations, exclude stablecoins/wrapped tokens, winsorize returns, and use a pre/post-2020 split around known data-quality problems. — [Borri et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)
- Alexander & Dakos (2020), "A critical investigation of cryptocurrency data and analysis", Quantitative Finance 20(2):173–188 (SSRN 3382828): warns against non-traded aggregator prices (CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap, CryptoCompare) for trading-strategy, efficiency and hedging research; results such as GARCH calibration are very sensitive to the data source; some exchange prices need adjusting for tether instability; Bitfinex prices decoupled from others. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3382828); [Sussex PDF](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/A_critical_investigation_of_cryptocurrency_data_and_analysis/23470910/1/files/41179505.pdf)
- Volume contamination by wash trading (> 70% on unregulated venues; DEX wash trading up to US$2.57bn in 2024). — [Cong et al.](https://nber.org/papers/w30783); [Chainalysis via Economy Middle East](https://economymiddleeast.com/news/suspected-wash-trading-blockchains-2-57-billion-2024-report)
- Data-construction errors can invalidate entire on-chain studies (pump.fun paper withdrawn because "buyer" records included sells). — [arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)

#### Pitfall 6 — Data snooping and multiple testing (remedies)
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", RFS 29(1):5–68, doi 10.1093/rfs/hhv059: with 316+ tested factors, a new factor needs t > 3.0. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059)
- Harvey & Liu (2015), "Backtesting", Journal of Portfolio Management 42(1):13–28 (haircut Sharpe ratios for multiple testing), doi 10.3905/jpm.2015.42.1.013; Harvey & Liu (2020), "False (and Missed) Discoveries in Financial Economics", JF 75(5):2503–2553, doi 10.1111/jofi.12951. — [DOI 2015](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2015.42.1.013); [DOI 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12951)
- White (2000), "A Reality Check for Data Snooping", Econometrica 68(5):1097–1126 — bootstrap test of whether the *best* of many rules beats a benchmark given the search; Hansen (2005), "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability", JBES 23(4):365–380 — studentized/recentred version less sensitive to adding poor rules; Romano & Wolf (2005), Econometrica 73(4):1237–1282 — stepwise procedure identifying *which* rules outperform with family-wise error control. — [White DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152); [Hansen DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063); [Romano–Wolf DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x)
- Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), JF 54(5):1647–1691: Reality Check applied to a universe of thousands of technical rules on ~100 years of DJIA data; the best rules' apparent superiority did not carry into the later out-of-sample period. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00163)
- Bailey & López de Prado (2014), "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio", JPM 40(5):94–107 — corrects the Sharpe ratio for the number of trials, non-normal returns and track-record length; Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2017), "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", Journal of Computational Finance 20(4) — combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) estimates the probability that the in-sample best configuration underperforms out-of-sample. — [DSR DOI](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094) / [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [PBO DOI](https://doi.org/10.21314/JCF.2016.322) / [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)
- Benjamini & Hochberg (1995), JRSS-B 57(1):289–300 — false discovery rate control for screening many coins/signals; Barras, Scaillet & Wermers (2010), JF 65(1):179–216 — FDR applied to separate skill from luck in fund alphas. — [BH DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x); [BSW DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01527.x)
- Replication/decay benchmarks from equities: McLean & Pontiff (2016), JF 71(1):5–32 — anomaly returns ~26% lower out-of-sample and ~58% lower post-publication; Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020), RFS 33(5):2019–2133 — with microcaps controlled (NYSE breakpoints, value weighting), 65% of 452 anomalies fail |t| ≥ 1.96 and 82% fail a multiple-testing hurdle of 2.78. — [McLean–Pontiff DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365); [HXZ DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhy131)
- Out-of-sample predictive evaluation: Welch & Goyal (2008), RFS 21(4):1455–1508 — most popular equity-premium predictors fail to beat the historical mean OOS; Campbell & Thompson (2008), RFS 21(4):1509–1531 — OOS R² vs. historical mean; even small positive OOS R² can be economically meaningful. — [Welch–Goyal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm014); [Campbell–Thompson DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm055)
- Crypto-specific: researcher degrees of freedom produce non-standard errors larger than standard errors (Fieberg et al. 2024); multiple-testing procedures applied to ~15,000 technical rules on four large coins (Hudson & Urquhart 2021). — [Fieberg et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v92y2024ics1057521924000383.html); [Hudson & Urquhart](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715)

#### Pitfall 7 — Non-stationarity and regime shifts in on-chain series
- Inscriptions/BRC-20/Runes changed what BTC activity metrics measure: active addresses fell while transaction counts rose (address reuse); Runes took 57.2% of daily transactions in mid-2024. — [Glassnode, The Week On-chain 24/2024](https://research.glassnode.com/the-week-onchain-week-24-2024/)
- BTC daily transactions hit a record in Sep 2023 and then dropped with Ordinals activity. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/255659/bitcoin-transactions-see-sharp-decline-following-ordinals-peak-hype)
- [IND/press] Commentary attributes the 2025–26 fall in BTC exchange reserves partly to moves into private wallets, ETF custody and long-term-holder addresses (a structural, not purely behavioural, change). It also notes that prices did not follow the reserve decline as the "low reserves = bullish" reading predicts. — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitcoin-exchanges-reserves-7-year-low-hedge-funds-buy-dip); [BlockEden summary of CryptoQuant data](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/23/bitcoin-exchange-balances-7-year-low-supply-shock-whale-accumulation/); [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/bitcoin-exchange-reserves-hit-a-record-low-so-why-isnt-the-price-rising-202512160924)
- The new-address value spread weakened from −0.035 (full) to −0.023 (post-2020) per week. — [Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4)

#### Pitfall 8 — Transaction costs and capacity
- Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016), "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs", RFS 29(1):104–147: many high-turnover anomalies are not profitable after realistic trading costs; cost-mitigation rules help. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063)
- Intraday on-chain flow effects explain ≈0.1–0.2% of return variance and minute-level whale effects are a few hundredths of a percent. — [Chi et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.06327); [Ante & Fiedler](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf)
- The P&D prediction strategy's 60% return was demonstrated only on small retail size, on coins with market caps often < 100 BTC. — [Xu & Livshits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10109)

#### Recommended research designs (as used in or recommended by the literature)
- **Event studies**: MacKinlay (1997), "Event Studies in Economics and Finance", JEL 35(1):13–39 (standard framework). When event dates cluster (crypto-wide moves on the same days), cross-correlated abnormal returns make standard tests over-reject — Kolari & Pynnönen (2010), RFS 23(11):3996–4025; event-induced variance — Boehmer, Musumeci & Poulsen (1991), JFE 30(2):253–272; nonparametric rank test — Corrado (1989), JFE 23(2):385–395; matched control samples for longer windows — Barber & Lyon (1997), JFE 43(3):341–372. — [JSTOR MacKinlay](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2729691); [Kolari–Pynnönen DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhq072); [BMP DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(91)90032-F); [Corrado DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(89)90064-0); [Barber–Lyon DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(96)00890-2)
- Crypto event-study practice: mean-return models with short estimation windows (2 h for minute data; 121 h for hourly) and explicit pre-event windows (−15 min; −24 h; pre-event week) to detect leakage. — [Ante & Fiedler](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Market-Reaction-to-Large-Transfers-on-the-Bitcoin-Blockchain-Do-Size-and-Motive-Matter-BRL-Working-Paper-9.pdf); [Ante, Fiedler & Strehle](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The_Influence_of_Stablecoin_Issuances_on_Cryptocurrency_Markets_BRL_Working_Paper_No_11.pdf)
- Pre-/post-announcement drift tests in event time around on-chain data releases. — [Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu WP](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/onchain_v18.pdf)
- **Matched controls + placebo diagnostics**: propensity-score matching (1:1 nearest neighbour, 0.2-SD caliper on launch-quality covariates) with an activity-matched placebo run over 100 seeds — whose placebo turned out biased (median +189.6%), illustrating why placebo distributions must be centred on zero before trusting the main estimate. — [Kamat arXiv 2607.02795 (results withdrawn)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)
- **Cross-sectional factor tests**: weekly portfolio sorts (formation week t, value-weighted returns in t+1), Q5−Q1 spreads, alphas against crypto C-3 (market, size, momentum) / C-4 (+value) models, Fama–MacBeth regressions. — [Borri et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14435v4); [Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu (2022) JF 77(2):1133–1177](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13119); [Fama & MacBeth (1973) JPE 81(3):607–636](https://doi.org/10.1086/260061); [Newey & West (1987) Econometrica 55(3):703–708](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913610)
- **Walk-forward / leakage control**: purged k-fold and embargo to stop overlapping labels leaking across train/test (López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley 2018). — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Advances+in+Financial+Machine+Learning-p-9781119482086)
- **Permutation / bootstrap inference preserving autocorrelation**: stationary bootstrap — Politis & Romano (1994), JASA 89(428):1303–1313; the Reality Check and SPA are bootstrap-based. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870)

### Inferences
**Statistical power with 1–2 years of data.** Since t ≈ annualized Sharpe × √(years), clearing t = 2 needs an annualized Sharpe ≈ 1.4 (2 years) or 2.0 (1 year); clearing the Harvey–Liu–Zhu hurdle t = 3 needs ≈ 2.1 (2 years) or 3.0 (1 year). For an event study, if per-event abnormal-return dispersion is ~15% (plausible for an altcoin over a week), detecting a −3% mean at t = 3 needs roughly (3 × 15 / 3)² ≈ 225 independent events. Fewer events, or events clustered on the same dates, need a larger effect.

**Checklist for one on-chain signal across a few hundred Binance-perp altcoins**
1. **Pre-specify and count trials.** Write the signal definition, horizon(s), universe, entry/exit and the full list of variants before looking at returns. Log every variant tried; the count feeds DSR, PBO, Reality Check/SPA and the Romano–Wolf family.
2. **Point-in-time data only.** Use PiT vendor series (with `computed_at`) or snapshot your own label sets with as-of dates. Never apply 2026 wallet labels (team, VC, exchange, Arkham tags) to 2024–25 events without an as-of-date audit. Treat low-confidence labels as noise.
3. **Lag for publication.** A vendor daily value for day D is not usable at D 00:00 UTC (Glassnode releases 01:10–02:40 UTC); BTC data enter metrics 30–60 min late. Compute signals from data available at decision time and execute on the next Binance bar, never the same bar's close.
4. **Clean the flows.** Remove exchange-internal hot↔cold moves and deposit-address sweeps; separate inter-exchange (arbitrage) flows from end-user flows; tag sources (vesting/unlock contracts, team/treasury, market makers, bridges). Measure all chains on which the token and its Binance deposit networks exist (ERC-20, BEP-20, Solana and others) and include bridges.
5. **Point-in-time universe.** Include every coin that had a Binance USDT-M perp at time t, including later-delisted contracts and dead tokens; keep their full price history; set minimum liquidity/market-cap filters at formation, not ex post. Report results with and without the smallest decile (non-standard errors live there).
6. **Use traded prices and honest volume.** Use Binance perp/spot traded prices, not aggregator indices. Do not use aggregator "volume" as a control or denominator without checking it for wash trading.
7. **Benchmark properly.** Report returns net of BTC/market beta. For cross-sections, report alpha against market–size–momentum (plus value). For events, build matched controls from same-date non-event coins with similar size, prior-30-day return, volatility, funding and open interest, because altcoin events cluster in time.
8. **Event-study mechanics.** Choose an estimation window that excludes the pre-event drift (unlock declines start ~30 days early). Test pre-event windows explicitly, since pre-event leakage is common (unlocks, listings, pumps). Use BMP/Kolari–Pynnönen-style or rank tests, cluster by date, and bootstrap confidence intervals.
9. **Placebos.** Run random pseudo-event dates for the same coins, random large-holder wallets in place of "team" wallets, and shuffled wallet labels. The placebo distribution must be centred near zero; if not, the estimator is biased.
10. **Walk-forward OOS.** Fit thresholds on, e.g., the first 12 months and test on the rest (or roll monthly), purging overlapping horizons with an embargo. Report OOS R² vs. a zero/historical-mean forecast and the decay from in-sample to OOS; expect the OOS effect to be smaller (equity analogue: ~26% decay OOS).
11. **Multiple testing.** Report White RC/Hansen SPA p-values for the best variant and Romano–Wolf for the set. Report the deflated Sharpe given the trial count and the PBO via CSCV. Use BH-FDR when screening coin-level or wallet-level signals. Treat t < 3 as unconfirmed.
12. **Permutation tests.** Circularly shift or block-permute the signal within each coin (preserving autocorrelation and the cross-section of returns) to build the null for the strategy's Sharpe or hit rate.
13. **Reverse-causality controls.** Add lagged returns, volatility and funding to every flow regression, because inflows follow price drops and volatility.
14. **Regime splits.** Show results by half-sample, by bull/bear and high/low-volatility state, and around structural breaks (ETF custody shifts, inscription/Runes waves, L2 migration, exchange-wallet migrations). Require sign stability.
15. **Costs, funding and capacity.** Deduct taker fees, spread or slippage scaled to order size vs. book depth, and funding paid or received over the holding period. Cap position size at a fraction of daily perp volume, and check capacity on the thinnest names where most "edge" usually sits. Intraday on-chain effects of 0.1–0.2% R² will rarely survive this.
16. **Pipeline audits.** Reconcile buys and sells and transfer counts against explorers, spot-check the top flows by hand, and re-run with an independently built dataset if possible. The withdrawn pump.fun study is the cautionary case.
17. **Report everything.** Publish the full distribution of variants, not just the winner, including null results.

### Gaps
- No academic paper was found that quantifies how much retroactive labelling inflates backtest performance for exchange-flow or whale signals; the evidence is vendor documentation (Glassnode).
- No study found that measures bias from bridges or multi-chain token deployments in on-chain flow metrics.
- No crypto-specific study found applying deflated Sharpe or PBO to on-chain strategies; the methods are generic.
- Binance-specific operational details (deposit-confirmation counts per network, internal-wallet migrations, delisted-perp data availability) were outside the academic scope searched and are not sourced here.
